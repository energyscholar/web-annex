Skeleton 4: Computation

Universality → Coordination. Bridge to Skeleton 2 at the critical point.

No ant has a map

An ant colony finds the shortest path to food. No ant knows the shortest path. No ant has a map. No ant is in charge. Each ant follows two rules: (1) follow pheromone trails, (2) leave pheromone behind.

From those two rules, the colony computes optimal foraging routes. The computation is distributed across thousands of simple agents. No individual agent computes anything useful. The system computes.

This is Skeleton 4: distributed computation. Simple local rules producing global information processing without any central controller.

Four rules, infinite complexity

Conway's Game of Life. A grid. Each cell is alive or dead. Four rules:

Rules

  1. Alive + fewer than 2 neighbors → dies (underpopulation)
  2. Alive + 2 or 3 neighbors → lives
  3. Alive + more than 3 neighbors → dies (overcrowding)
  4. Dead + exactly 3 neighbors → becomes alive

What emerges

Gliders (move across the grid). Oscillators (repeat). Glider guns (produce gliders). Logic gates. A Turing-complete computer. From four rules about counting neighbors.

Left: a glider (moves diagonally). Right: a blinker (oscillates in place).

The key insight: the rules are trivial. The behavior is not. The gap between rule complexity and behavior complexity is where computation lives.

Edge of chaos = the critical point

Not all rule sets produce interesting computation. Most produce one of two outcomes:

Ordered

❄

Everything freezes. Information can't flow. Structures are static. No computation.

Critical

⚡

Long-range correlations. Information flows freely. Complex structures form AND dissolve. Computation concentrates here.

Chaotic

🔥

Everything fluctuates. Information is destroyed by noise. No stable structures. No computation.

Langton (1990) showed this explicitly: computational capability peaks at the phase transition between order and chaos. Too ordered = frozen, can't compute. Too chaotic = noisy, can't remember. The sweet spot is the critical point.

The bridge to Skeleton 2

This is where Skeleton 4 (Computation) meets Skeleton 2 (Dynamics/Criticality). The edge of chaos IS the critical point. Computation concentrates at criticality. This is not a metaphor — it's the same mathematical object seen from two directions.

Computation adds a new lens to criticality: the critical point isn't just where phase transitions happen. It's where information processing is maximized. The system computes most powerfully at exactly the point where it's about to change state.

Two depths of computation

Depth 1: Universality

The system can simulate any computation, given enough time and space. Conway's Life is Turing-complete. Random Boolean networks at criticality are Turing-complete. A system at depth 1 has the capacity for arbitrary computation.

Universality is about potential: the computational substrate is powerful enough, in principle, to compute anything. Whether it actually does is a separate question.

Depth 2: Coordination

The system doesn't just have computational capacity — it uses it to coordinate behavior across scales. 86 billion neurons don't just fire randomly at criticality. They coordinate into perception, decision, action. Millions of ants don't just diffuse randomly. They coordinate into foraging, nest building, defense.

Coordination is universality harnessed. The gap between "can compute" and "does compute something useful" is the gap between depth 1 and depth 2.

Many systems have universality (depth 1). Fewer achieve coordination (depth 2). A cellular automaton at criticality has universality. A brain at criticality has coordination. An ML network at double descent has coordination. The difference is: depth 2 systems have functional computation, not just potential computation.

The brain computes at criticality

Neuronal avalanches follow power laws. This was one of the early criticality observations (Beggs & Plenz, 2003). The brain operates near a critical point.

But the brain doesn't just exist at criticality. It computes there. The critical point maximizes:

Dynamic range

The range of stimulus intensities the system can distinguish. Maximized at criticality. Subcritical systems compress dynamic range. Supercritical systems saturate.

Information transfer

Mutual information between distant neurons. Maximized at criticality. The critical point is where correlations span the whole system, enabling long-range coordination.

The brain has both depth 1 (universality — it can, in principle, compute anything) and depth 2 (coordination — it actually coordinates perception, thought, and action). It's the prototypical Skeleton 4 system.

Eight fields, one framework

Distributed computation was discovered and rediscovered across at least eight fields:

1936
Turing
Universal computation. Abstract machines. Proved limits of computability.
1948
von Neumann
Cellular automata. Self-reproducing machines. Showed computation could be distributed.
1970
Conway
Game of Life. Proved simple local rules can produce universal computation.
1987
Bak, Tang, Wiesenfeld
Self-organized criticality. Systems that tune themselves to the computational sweet spot.
1990
Langton
Edge of chaos. Explicitly showed computation peaks at the phase transition.
1993
Kauffman
Boolean networks. Random gene regulatory networks compute at criticality.
2000s
Reservoir computing
Echo state networks. Showed that any sufficiently complex dynamical system near criticality can compute.
2002
Wolfram
A New Kind of Science. Classified all simple programs. Class 4 (between order and chaos) = universal computation.

Mathematics → automata → game theory → statistical physics → complex systems → biology → machine learning → computational theory. Eight approaches to the same insight: complex computation arises at the boundary between order and chaos.

Recap

Skeleton
Distributed computation / universality — simple local rules producing global information processing without central control.
Depth 1: Universality
The system has the capacity for arbitrary computation. Turing-complete. The substrate is powerful enough.
Depth 2: Coordination
The system uses its computational capacity to coordinate behavior across scales. Perception, foraging, learning. Computation harnessed.
Bridge to Skeleton 2
Edge of chaos = critical point. Computation concentrates at criticality. This is the CB bridge generalized: Skeleton 4 and Skeleton 2 are the same mathematics seen from two directions.
Key insight
You can't compute in a frozen system (no information flow) or a chaotic one (no memory). The critical point is where computation lives.
Convergence
8 fields independently arrived at distributed computation at the critical point. Turing, von Neumann, Conway, Bak, Langton, Kauffman, reservoir computing, Wolfram.
