Work in progress. This is exploratory research into multicriticality. Multicritical points themselves are a well-established topic in statistical physics, and a recent literature actively studies how systems self-organize toward them (e.g. Sormunen, Gross & Saramäki 2025, arXiv:2506.04275). What is provisional here is the cross-system synthesis: the skeleton assignments, the pairwise mechanism claims, and the system-by-system profiles are hypotheses developed through computational experiments and literature review. They likely contain errors. The goal is to make the questions visible, not to present finished answers.

Multi-Critical Systems

Six physical systems where independent phase transitions converge

This is a series of six interactive tutorials examining a pattern that appears across condensed matter, biophysics, neuroscience, and plasma physics: multiple independent phase transitionsSudden qualitative changes in a system's behavior at threshold values — like water freezing or magnets losing magnetism. Characterized by power laws, diverging correlations, and universal behavior. converging in a single physical system. At the convergence point, the transitions couple, the universality classA category of phase transitions sharing the same critical exponents regardless of microscopic details. Water boiling and iron demagnetizing share a universality class — different physics, identical mathematics at the transition. changes, and new structure emerges that no single transition contains.

A single critical transition is a point in parameter space where one order parameterA measurable quantity that distinguishes phases. Zero on one side of the transition, nonzero on the other. Magnetization for magnets, density difference for liquids. At criticality, it fluctuates at all scales. diverges — the system reorganizes. This is well understood. A multi-critical pointA point in parameter space where two or more independent phase transitions converge. The physics at this point is qualitatively different from either transition alone — new exponents, new correlations, new phenomena. is where two or more independent transitions converge. At the coupling point, the physics changes qualitatively: the coupled system produces correlations, failure modes, and early-warning signals that exist only in the interaction between transitions, not in either one alone.

Each tutorial below teaches one physical system, starting from plain language and building to the multi-critical structure. Technical detail, falsification criteria, and citations are available in expandable sections for readers who want depth. The tutorials are ordered from simplest (two transitions, one coupling parameter) to most complex (four independent frameworks converging), and form three natural pairs connected by shared physical mechanisms.

The central question: why do multiple independent transitions converge in the same physical system — and what does the coupling produce that no single transition contains?

2. Three Pairs

The six systems form three natural pairs, each connected by a shared physical mechanism that crosses domain boundaries. These are not analogies. In each pair, the same mathematical structure appears in both systems with measurable, quantitatively comparable signatures.

Glass ↔ Protein

The Gardner transitionA secondary transition within the glass phase where the energy landscape shatters from smooth basins into a fractal hierarchy of sub-basins. Discovered theoretically in spin glasses, now confirmed experimentally in colloids. sits below the Kauzmann temperature. Gardner produces jamming scalings. Jamming sets the protein packing fraction φc ≈ 0.55. The chain runs: glass bicriticality → Gardner → jamming → protein packing. Two systems, one causal chain through three transitions.

Tutorials 01 & 04

Active Matter ↔ Cardiac

Both systems contain topological defects that unbind through Kosterlitz-Thouless physics. In active nematics: ordered → periodic braiding → chaotic. In cardiac tissue: sinus rhythm → flutter (bound vortices) → fibrillation (unbound). Same three-regime structure, same topological mechanism, different substrates.

Tutorials 02 & 03

Brain ↔ Tokamak

Both are modular-hierarchical systems where the architecture stretches a critical point into a Griffiths phase. In the brain: cortical modules create an extended critical phase without fine-tuning. In the tokamak: nested transport barriers create a self-sustaining critical region. Same architectural principle, same consequence — robust criticality from structure, not parameter tuning.

5. The Question

Each of these systems is well-studied within its home domain. Cardiac electrophysiologists know the Feigenbaum cascade. Active matter physicists know the golden braid. Glass physicists know the two transitions. Plasma physicists know the SOC pedestal.

What none of these communities has asked is: why do multiple independent transitions converge in the same physical system? And what does the coupling between transitions produce that no single transition contains? Recent work on self-organization to multicriticalityA 2025 line of work (Sormunen, Gross & Saramäki) showing that some systems tune themselves toward multicritical points without external fine-tuning — a possible mechanism for why such points recur across domains. (Sormunen, Gross & Saramäki 2025) begins to ask a related question from the dynamics side.

That is what these tutorials examine.
