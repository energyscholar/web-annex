What is a braid?

Three strands hanging vertically. When two cross, that's a braid generator. The crossing IS the information — not the material the strands are made of, not their thickness, not their color. Just: which crossed over which.

Identity
No crossings. Nothing happens.

Left pair crosses.
Blue over red.
σ₂
Right pair crosses.

Over and under matter

These two look almost the same. They're not. In σ₁, blue goes over red. In σ₁¹, red goes over blue. Different braids. Different topology. You cannot smoothly deform one into the other.

σ₁
Blue over red
σ₁⁻¹
Red over blue

This is why topology is powerful: the crossing direction is a discrete, absolute fact that survives any continuous deformation. You can stretch, wiggle, or compress the strands — the over/under pattern doesn't change.

Braids compose

Stack crossings top to bottom. Two crossings make a braid word. Three make a longer word. The word encodes the complete braiding pattern.

Two critical braid words, both with two crossings, but with very different futures:

σ₁σ₂ — same direction

Both crossings go the same way (left strand over right). The strands rotate around each other.

σ₁σ₂⁻¹ — alternating

Crossings alternate direction (left over, then right over). The strands fold over each other.

Periodic vs. pseudo-Anosov

Extend each pattern. Watch what happens.

Periodic: σ₁σ₂σ₁σ₂σ₁σ₂

After 3 full cycles, the strands return to their starting positions. This braid repeats. TEPO = 0.

Golden: σ₁σ₂⁻¹σ₁σ₂⁻¹σ₁σ₂⁻¹

The strands never return. Each cycle stretches the pattern further. This is pseudo-Anosov. TEPO = log(φ).

The periodic braid is like twisting a rope — it winds up but can unwind. The golden braid is like kneading dough — each fold builds on the previous one, and you can never unfold it back.

Where the golden ratio comes from

Imagine stretching taffy with three hooks. Each σ₁ folds the taffy one way. Each σ₂⁻¹ folds it the opposite way. Because the folds alternate direction, each new fold builds on the previous two folds, not just the last one.

1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, 34 — the Fibonacci numbers. Each is the sum of the previous two. The ratio of consecutive Fibonacci numbers converges to φ = 1.618...

The golden ratio appears because alternating braid crossings create Fibonacci growth. Each fold reaches back two steps, satisfying F(n) = F(n-1) + F(n-2). The growth rate of that recursion is φ.

TEPO = log(φ) ≈ 0.481 is the minimum possible stretching rate for a non-repeating 3-strand braid. Less than log(φ) means the braid is periodic. At or above log(φ) means it's genuinely complex (pseudo-Anosov). The golden ratio is the exact boundary between simple and complex braiding.

Topological protection

Now the physical payoff. Look at this braid:

wiggle, stretch, compress...

I'm deforming it. Wiggling the strands, stretching them, compressing them. The crossing pattern doesn't change. Blue still goes over red at the top. Red still goes over green at the bottom. The topology — the over/under sequence — survives any smooth deformation.

If you store information in that crossing pattern, the information is protected against local noise. Small perturbations can't change it. Thermal fluctuations can't change it. The only way to change the crossing pattern is to cut a strand — a drastic, non-local action.

This is Depth 1: Protection. The system has topological properties that resist local perturbation. Topological insulators work this way — their surface conduction is protected by topology, not by material purity.

Topological computation

If crossing patterns can encode information, can they encode computation?

Yes. Kitaev (1997) showed that braiding certain particles — anyons — around each other performs quantum logic gates. The computation is encoded in the braid pattern, and the braid pattern is topologically protected. Errors can't creep in because errors are local, and the computation is global.

This is Depth 2: Fault-tolerant computation. Not just protecting stored information, but protecting active computation. The system doesn't just resist noise — it thinks through it.

Not all anyons can do this. Ising anyons (k=2) are too simple. Fibonacci anyons (k=3) are the simplest that achieve computational universality. Their quantum dimension is φ — the same golden ratio, from the same braid group algebra.

Where φ appears

The golden ratio is the tracer for this skeleton. When φ shows up in a physical system, it means B₃ braiding is active. Four fields found it independently:

Active Nematics

TEPO = log(φ)

Liquid crystal defects braid in confined geometries. The stretching rate of defect trajectories = log(φ). Measured computationally. Geometry selects the braid class.

Fibonacci Anyons

d = φ

Quantum dimension of Fibonacci anyons = φ. From the fusion algebra of SU(2) at level k=3. The simplest non-abelian anyon with computational universality.

Quasicrystals

ratio = φ

Long-to-short tile ratio in Penrose tilings = φ. From the algebraic structure of 5-fold symmetry, connected to the icosahedral group.

Cardiac (predicted)

TEPO = log(φ)?

If spiral wave tips in the heart braid according to B₃, the golden ratio should appear in their trajectories. Untested. This is an open falsification target.

Four fields. Same number. Same algebra underneath. No shared citations between the first three discoveries. φ is a binary test: it either shows up or it doesn't. That makes this skeleton the most falsifiable of the four.

Seven communities, one mathematics

Like criticality (FACETS), topological mathematics was independently rediscovered by communities that don't read each other's journals:

1860s+
Mathematicians
Knot theory, braid groups, Jones polynomial. Pure abstraction.
1970s
Thouless, Haldane, Kosterlitz
Topological phase transitions. Condensed matter physics. Nobel 2016.
1982
Laughlin, Tsui, Störmer
Fractional quantum Hall effect. Found topological order in a 2D electron gas.
1988
Witten
Topological quantum field theory. String theory. Connected topology to physics axiomatically.
1997
Kitaev
Topological quantum computation. Realized braiding = fault-tolerant quantum gates.
2005
Kane & Mele
Topological insulators. A new class of materials with topologically protected surface states.
2010s
Active matter researchers
Topological defects govern dynamics in liquid crystals, biological tissues, bacterial colonies.

The mathematicians didn't teach the physicists. The condensed matter people didn't teach the computer scientists. The active matter people came at it from biology. Seven approaches to the same mathematics, arriving from seven directions.
