Cardiac Tissue

Period-doubling, defect unbinding, and autocatalytic closure — in one organ

Your heart beats about 100,000 times a day. Each beat is a coordinated electrical wave that sweeps across the atria, pauses at the atrioventricular nodeThe electrical relay between the upper chambers (atria) and lower chambers (ventricles) of the heart. It briefly delays the signal so the ventricles fill before they contract., then triggers the ventricles. When this coordination fails, the result ranges from harmless palpitations to sudden cardiac death. The physics of how it fails turns out to involve three independent transition frameworks converging in a single tissue.

No other system in this series makes the multi-critical structure so clinically visible. Each transition has been observed in human patients, each has its own diagnostic signature, and the coupling between them has direct implications for treatment.

Three Transitions in One Tissue

1. The Feigenbaum Cascade — a period-doubling route to chaos

In 2023, Cantwell and colleagues observed a full Feigenbaum period-doubling cascadeA universal route to chaos where a system’s behavior successively doubles in complexity: period-1 → period-2 → period-4 → period-8 → chaos. Discovered by Mitchell Feigenbaum in 1978. The same pattern appears in dripping faucets, population dynamics, and lasers. in human cardiac tissue immediately before ventricular fibrillationA lethal rhythm in which the heart’s lower chambers quiver chaotically instead of pumping. Without immediate defibrillation it causes sudden cardiac death within minutes..1 The electrical activation pattern went from period-1 (normal rhythm) to period-2 (alternansA beat-to-beat alternation in the heart’s electrical signal — one beat is long, the next is short, repeating. Clinically recognized as an early warning of dangerous arrhythmias. — every other beat is different) to period-4 and period-8, then into chaotic fibrillation.

This is more than a loose metaphor. The cascade appears to follow the Feigenbaum universal constantThe number δ = 4.669... that governs the ratio of successive bifurcation intervals in all period-doubling cascades. It is as fundamental to chaos theory as π is to circles — the same constant appears in every system that follows this route to chaos. δ = 4.669...: the ratios of successive bifurcation intervals reported in the 2023 measurements are consistent with the universal value, though a single dataset cannot pin the constant down to high precision.2 The same mathematics that governs the transition to chaos in dripping faucets, population dynamics, and laser intensity fluctuations appears to govern the breakdown of cardiac rhythm.

2. KT Defect Unbinding — a topological transition

Independently of the Feigenbaum cascade, the spatial pattern of electrical activity undergoes a Kosterlitz-Thouless (KT) transitionA topological phase transition where bound vortex-antivortex pairs unbind. Below the transition: pairs orbit each other in stable configurations. Above it: free vortices proliferate. Nobel Prize in Physics, 2016.. At low activity: spiral wavesRotating electrical waves that spiral outward from a central tip. In cardiac tissue, each spiral tip creates a local reentrant circuit. Bound spiral pairs are manageable; free-roaming spiral tips are dangerous. are bound in pairs, orbiting each other in stable configurations. This corresponds clinically to atrial flutter — a fast but organized rhythm. At higher activity: the pairs unbind. Free spiral tips proliferate and interact chaotically. This is atrial fibrillation.

Ganesan and colleagues (2025) showed that targeting the topological boundaries — the regions where bound pairs separate — terminates arrhythmias with 75–96% success rates.3 The treatment works because it respects the topological structure: destroy the boundary where defects unbind via catheter ablationA clinical procedure where a thin wire (catheter) is threaded into the heart and used to burn small lines in the tissue, creating barriers that block abnormal electrical pathways. The physics equivalent of drawing a wall across the defect unbinding boundary., and the transition from flutter to fibrillation cannot occur.

3. RAF Closure — an autocatalytic transition

The calcium signaling network that drives each heartbeat is a reflexively autocatalytic and food-generated (RAF) setA network where every reaction is catalyzed by products of other reactions in the network, and raw inputs come from outside. Originally developed for origin-of-life chemistry — the same mathematics describes how life bootstraps itself from simple molecules..4 This is a concept from origin-of-life chemistry: a network where every reaction’s catalysts are produced by other reactions in the network, and the raw inputs come from the environment.

The cardiac calcium network scores 17 out of 17 reactions in the RAF — a maximal RAFAn RAF where every possible reaction participates — nothing is left out. A maximal RAF has the most complete autocatalytic closure possible for the given network.. And it has a remarkable structural property: zero individually essential reactions.◆ our resultAn original, not-yet-peer-reviewed result from this research program — distinct from established consensus. Remove any single reaction, and the remaining 16 still form a functioning RAF. The system has complete single-fault toleranceThe ability to lose any one component and keep working. In engineering, this is N+1 redundancy. In the cardiac RAF, every reaction has a backup path — the network routes around any single failure..

The Unbreakable Network

The third framework is the most surprising. The calcium signaling cascade that triggers each heartbeat — L-type calcium channels, the ryanodine receptor, SERCA pumps, calmodulin, protein kinase A — forms a complete RAF: every reaction in the network is catalyzed by products of other reactions in the network, using only calcium ions from the extracellular space as raw input.

RAF analysis reveals a striking structural property. Of the 17 reactions, none is individually essential.◆ our resultAn original, not-yet-peer-reviewed result from this research program — distinct from established consensus. Remove any single reaction — any ion channel, any kinase, any pump — and the remaining 16 still form a functioning autocatalytic set. The system has evolved not just to function, but to be robust against single-point failures.

Where does it break? Only at the class level. Remove all calcium entry pathways simultaneously — block L-type channels, T-type channels, and sodium-calcium exchangers together — and the RAF collapses from 17 reactions to zero. The network has no root reaction, but it has a root class: external calcium entry. This is why calcium channel blockers are clinically effective while targeting individual channels rarely is.

The minimum sustaining core is just three reactions: a PKA phosphorylation cycle.◆ our resultAn original, not-yet-peer-reviewed result from this research program — distinct from established consensus. This 3-reaction core maintains the entire autocatalytic set. It is the smallest RAF within the RAF — the kernel that everything else depends on.

How the Three Transitions Couple

Each transition framework describes something real in cardiac tissue. But they are not three independent stories about the same organ. They interact:

The Feigenbaum cascade is the temporal route to chaos — it describes how the beat-to-beat timing becomes unpredictable. The KT unbinding is the spatial route to chaos — it describes how the wavefront pattern becomes disorganized. The cascade triggers the unbinding: period-doubling creates the spatial heterogeneity that nucleates spiral wave pairs. As the cascade progresses, the pairs unbind.

The RAF is the substrate that supports both. It persists through the entire transition sequence. The calcium network remains autocatalytic whether the heart is in sinus rhythm, flutter, or fibrillation. The RAF does not cause the transitions — it provides the robust biochemical engine on which the transitions operate.

This is the multi-critical structure: temporal criticality (Feigenbaum), spatial criticality (KT), and network criticality (RAF closure), all present simultaneously, each with measurable signatures, interacting to produce the full clinical picture of cardiac arrhythmia. Recent work on classifying tipping points by their temporal scale-invariance (Frank & Jacobs, 2026) places period-doubling cascades like this one within a broader taxonomy of transitions — a reminder that the routes to arrhythmia are instances of much more general phenomena.

◆ Technical Detail: KT Unbinding and Clinical Ablation

The Kosterlitz-Thouless transition describes the unbinding of topological defect pairs. In 2D XY models: below TKT, vortex-antivortex pairs are bound (logarithmic confining potential). Above TKT, they unbind (free vortices, algebraic correlations → exponential).

Ganesan et al. (2025) demonstrated that cardiac spiral waves undergo precisely this transition. Atrial flutter maps to the bound-pair phase: two spiral tips orbit each other in a stable macro-reentrant circuit. Atrial fibrillation maps to the unbound phase: free spiral tips proliferate, creating the chaotic wavefront pattern seen on clinical electrograms.

The clinical intervention is topological: catheter ablation creates lesion lines that bisect the tissue region where unbinding occurs. The 75–96% success rate for arrhythmia termination correlates with how precisely the ablation targets the topological boundary between bound and unbound phases. Ablation that targets the boundary succeeds; ablation that targets spiral tips or anatomical landmarks has lower success.

◆ Technical Detail: RAF Analysis of the Calcium Network

A Reflexively Autocatalytic and Food-generated (RAF) set is a set R of reactions where: (1) every reaction in R is catalyzed by at least one molecule produced by reactions in R or present in the food set F, and (2) every reactant of every reaction in R is either in F or produced by reactions in R. The food set is the external input — for the cardiac network, this is extracellular calcium.

Results: maxRAF = 17/17 (all reactions). maxCAF (constructively autocatalytic) = 17/17. Essential reactions: 0. The RAF is fully redundant at the single-reaction level. Minimum sustaining core: 3 reactions (PKA phosphorylation cycle: R14_PKA_phos, R15_PP1_dephos, R16_feedback).

The structure is Griffiths-like: no single reaction is essential, but disruption propagates from root-level (class removal of all Ca entry) through the network in a cascade. The cascade structure predicts which drug combinations will produce catastrophic failure vs. manageable degradation.

Falsification: Prediction #3 — Cardiac RAF Is Maximal

Claim: The calcium signaling network that governs cardiac excitation-contraction coupling forms a maximal RAF with zero individually essential reactions. Catastrophic failure occurs only at class-level removal (all calcium entry pathways blocked simultaneously).

Status: Survived. maxRAF = 17/17. Zero essential reactions confirmed. RAF collapses 17 → 0 only when all Ca entry pathways are removed simultaneously. Minimal 3-reaction PKA core identified (novel finding — not previously reported in the cardiac modeling literature).

Significance: This finding connects cardiac physiology to origin-of-life chemistry through shared mathematical structure. The RAF formalism was developed for prebiotic chemical networks; its applicability to cardiac calcium signaling was not known prior to this analysis.

KILL CRITERIA: If the cardiac calcium network has essential reactions (removing one collapses the RAF), or if class-level removal does not produce catastrophic failure (gradual degradation instead), Prediction #3 fails. The RAF structure must be genuinely maximal and the class-level vulnerability must be real.

◆ Detection Question

Three order parameters on partially overlapping node sets. The Feigenbaum cascade is temporal (beat-to-beat APD sequence at each spatial point). The KT unbinding is spatial (spiral wave tip positions across the tissue surface). The RAF is biochemical (reaction rates in the calcium signaling network, same spatial domain but different node set — molecular species rather than tissue locations).

The Feigenbaum and KT transitions share a spatial node set but have different order parameters. The RAF operates on a different node set entirely (reactions and molecular species vs. tissue points). This is the cross-topology case: two topologies that do not share nodes, coupled through the physics.

The question: Can the transition from flutter to fibrillation (KT unbinding in the spatial field) be detected earlier by monitoring the temporal field (Feigenbaum period-doubling) for the onset of cross-component coupling? If the relational gradient operator on the temporal field shows a growing cross-component signal before the spatial field shows unbinding, the coupling provides lead time that single-field monitoring cannot.
