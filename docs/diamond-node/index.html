Domain Convergence Research

Investigating cross-domain bridges between eleven scientific fields

This collection documents a systematic investigation into structural relationships between criticality, topology, autocatalysis, computation, and seven other scientific domains. The work combines interactive tutorials with computational experiments, tracking both positive findings and null results.

Anyon Family Sweep
Which non-abelian anyon families support complex systems? Systematic evaluation of SU(2)k, SU(3)k, SO(3)k, D(S3), and Haagerup categories for self-catalysis, RAF closure, and computational universality. Finding: sharp phase transition at k=2 to k=3.

Experiment
Braiding Phase Space Search
NKS enumeration of 1,364 braiding rules on Fibonacci anyons, classified by Wolfram's four complexity classes. Finding: unitary evolution on finite Hilbert space cannot produce genuine chaos—all "Class IV" candidates are quasi-periodic.

Experiment
Lattice Braiding Search
Spatially extended braiding dynamics on 60-anyon chains. 1,570 simulations across 12 spatial patterns. Finding: product-state approximation cannot capture genuine complexity—it lives in quantum entanglement.

Key Findings

  • Self-catalysis and RAF closure are generic for k≥3 in SU(2)k Chern-Simons theory, not special to Fibonacci anyons. Verified by explicit construction of fusion rules for k=2 through k=6.
  • Sharp phase transition at k=2 to k=3: self-catalysis and computational universality share the same algebraic root (non-trivial self-coupling Njjj ≥ 1). Below threshold, neither property holds; at or above, both hold.
  • Fibonacci anyons sit at the edge of chaos for fusion algebras: minimal self-catalysis, minimal universality, golden ratio dimension growth. Analogous to K=2 in Boolean networks.
  • No genuine Class IV behavior in braiding phase space: unitary evolution on finite Hilbert space is linear and norm-preserving, giving Lyapunov exponent exactly zero. The 360 "Class IV" candidates (n=3) are quasi-periodic with irrational eigenvalue phases.
  • Bridge mechanism taxonomy: approximately 4 of 7 identified bridges operate on rule-space structural identity rather than parameter coincidence or dynamical coupling.
  • The AC bridge (topology ↔ autocatalysis) has physical content beyond the algebraic structural identity. Anyon emergence from the substrate (Nayak et al. 2008) IS physical generation in Kauffman's sense — the food set is energy input, the generation is collective excitation, the closure is fusion algebra. FQHE systems are already AC demonstrators; the gap is formal recognition and cross-community publication, not finding a new system.
