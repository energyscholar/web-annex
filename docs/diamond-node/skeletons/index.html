The Four Mathematical Skeletons

Independent mathematical structures that recur across physical systems

Across eleven scientific domains, the same four mathematical frameworks appear independently. Each was discovered multiple times by communities that don't read each other's journals. Each produces two emergent propertiesProperties that arise from the interaction of components but don't exist in any individual component. The whole is different from the sum of its parts. at different depths: a structural property ("has") and a functional property ("does"). Together they decompose the multi-critical profileThe combination of independent phase transitions present in a single system. Different systems have different profiles — some have two transitions, others three or four. of any physical system.

How They Work Together

Individual skeletons produce useful properties. When two or more are present in the same physical system, qualitatively new properties emerge that none has alone. The pairwise interactions produce self-organized criticality (S2×S3), topological quantum computation (S1×S4), and Turing universalityA system that can simulate any computation given enough time and space. Conway's Game of Life, the brain at criticality, and random Boolean networks all have this property. at the edge of chaos (S2×S4). The S1×S2 edge is made of something older than any of them: broken symmetry. A symmetry breaks — that is the dynamics — and the order parameter it leaves behind has a shape whose defects are the topology. On that reading it is not a fifth structure but the substance of one edge.
