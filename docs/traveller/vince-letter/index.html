Garoo paid this quarter in a new currency. The old one is not money any more. Three brokers in Loka refused it outright; the fourth took it and is regretting it publicly.

Vince says they will sort themselves out as they always have, and we should keep loading and settle later — which is, to be fair, what we have always done. Councillor Fost says we are giving away a harvest for paper.

First time I have thought Fost had a point. I have not said so to anyone but you.

— M.A.

2 of 3 — Vince Aliyev · sent at his own cost

Friends,

Four Garoovian warships hold station over Loka where the whole city can see them. Their commander read me a statement. It was not a negotiation. The food ships will be loaded on the usual schedule, and payment will be made in the new currency of the Provisional Authority.

I told her no bank here will take it. She said she understood, and read the statement again. I believe she did understand, and that she was not permitted to say anything else.

Then she named Power One. Not our cities, not our people — the fusion station that lights the peninsula. She said she hoped it would not be necessary. It is a very careful sort of threat, made by someone given no other instrument.

Now hear the part the offworld journals will get wrong. We were going to send that food anyway. We have sent Garoo our surplus every season for three hundred years; there is nowhere else for it to go. Nobody here has gone hungry. The problem is that nobody has paid us.

She has offered paper, and some goods that were not nothing but were not much. I think she was sent here without money and told not to come back empty.

We can carry this about a year. After that the farmers stop planting the surplus, and no speech of mine will change that arithmetic. So it is not a famine and it is not next month. It is a debt with a year-long fuse.

One other thing, which I put here because I am tired. Garoo has never sold us a starship. Not once, at any price, and I have asked three times in my life. They build them for half the subsector. I used to think it was money. It is not money. Now four of theirs sit over my head and I understand the policy better than I did.

So I am asking. I have never asked you for anything.

Bring warships. Bring whatever you have or can borrow. You have friends in the Imperial service and I do not. Perhaps one hull standing between them and the harbour makes somebody in that fleet ask a question they have not been asked.

And if I am wrong, come anyway and tell me what to do instead. I would rather be corrected by you than right on my own.

Your land is safe. Your compound is untouched. Thomas and Bob are well, and the Aslan pair watch the approaches and will not be talked out of it.

Vince Aliyev
Raschev
3 of 3 — appended · not shown to the sender

He will not write the part that matters, so I will.

When Garoo wanted you, they came to him. They had names, they had the warrant, and they had a great deal to offer a small world. He sat with them two hours and said he had never heard of you. He has never mentioned it and would be furious that I have. He does not think of it as something you owe him.

Now the part that is mine. Their commander asked to see me alone. Not my uncle — me, by written request, so that it could be refused quietly.

She did not read me a statement. She asked what Raschev wants — not what we would settle for; she was particular about the difference. She asked twice, as though she expected an answer ready.

Then she told me she has nothing to give. No money, no goods worth the name, and no authority to agree to anything not on her list. Three requests home, two acknowledgements, no instructions.

So she is not what the statement made her sound like. She is bargaining because she must bring something home, and she does not know what with, and she was asking a farmer's niece because there was no one else to ask.

I have not told my uncle. He would go and shout at her, and she is the only person up there who has asked us a question.

— Marta
M. Aliyev — third convoy; their crews are coming down≈3 months

Third convoy loaded, fourth requested. Vince said yes before the Council could meet, which Fost is calling a coup and which was probably right done wrongly.

Their crews have started coming down on shore leave, of all things. They pay in that currency and the shopkeepers take it now, because refusing an armed man's money is different from refusing a banker's. Some of them are very young. One apologised to me.

Thomas has asked twice for permission to use his comms. I have said no twice. I am no longer sure I am right.

— M.A.

Courier is loading, so: nothing has changed, which is its own kind of news. The ships are still there. The fourth convoy went up.

— M.A.
