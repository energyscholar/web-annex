The Unmasking of Garoo

For ninety years the Health Directorate governed a world on the principle that sickness was a choice, and therefore a crime. It did not fall to an invasion, a famine, or a war. It fell because a great many of the wrong people got a rash.

By Dr. Halle Ostrom, Investigative Fellow · Institute of Polity Studies, University of Mora
Filed from Garoo Highport (0130)

In brief — for those who will read no further

  • Garoo's Health Directorate is gone. Ninety years of arresting people for being visibly ill, ended in under eight months.
  • It was not overthrown. It stopped being obeyed.
  • It began with a rash — several thousand cases, all among the ruling class, all within nine days. Not contagious. Never explained.
  • Faced with a visibly ill governing class, the Health Police arrested nobody — while continuing to detain people in the lower districts for eczema, shaving rash, and in one confirmed case, freckles.
  • The population's response was to put on masks, which were illegal for everyone except the Health Police.
  • Roughly 400 dead, the great majority of them Health Police. A provisional council of forty-one now governs, and agrees on very little.
  • For visiting shipmasters: Garoo is no longer the world it was. The vitality statutes are suspended, the starport is being run by a mercenary co-operative on its own initiative, and there is no functioning customs regime. What replaces the Directorate is not yet decided, and some of the candidates are worse.

An affliction with no vector

Early last year a skin condition appeared in the upper terraces of Garoo's three arcologies. Trivial — inflamed lesions on face, neck and hands, six to eleven weeks, resolving untreated. Three things about it matter. It was not infectious — no vector was ever established, and it never reached the service levels, though those populations share air and water with the households affected. Onset was simultaneous, across unconnected households inside nine days; a clinician now on the council's medical board called it "less like a contagion than like a clock going off." And it was confined by class: Directorate officers, senior administrators, the mercantile families, the Autarch's court. Several thousand people. Nobody else.

The popular explanation, then and now, is diet. It does not account for the timing, and nobody offering it to me has tried to make it. The Directorate's inquiry listed five theories: diet, water reticulation, an industrial contaminant, a consignment of offworld goods that had cleared customs some weeks earlier, and sabotage. The first four were closed as unproductive. Only the fifth was pursued, and I will come to what was done with it. I have requested the customs manifests four times and been refused four times, most recently by an office that no longer exists.

The law that was never for them

Faced with several thousand visibly ill members of the governing class, the Directorate did nothing. No detentions in the upper terraces. No certificates revoked. No exemption announced, no statute amended, and no explanation given.

For three generations this world was taught that illness was moral failure. Then the entire apparatus of that teaching stood in public with its face visibly broken out, and arrested nobody.

The masking

Face coverings were prohibited to the general population — concealing your face concealed your condition, and was itself an offence. The sole exception was the Directorate's senior officers, whose masks were a badge of rank.

So the governing class had two options: break the law, or claim the privilege. Many claimed the privilege — masks appeared on merchants, administrators, the Autarch's cousins — and the Health Police, who had arrested a woman in Arco-3 that month for a covered cheek, said nothing.

On the ninth of the fifth month the shift crews leaving the Arco-2 manufactories came out masked. Perhaps a third of them. They carried nothing and chanted nothing; they walked home. The Directorate arrested a hundred and forty. The following shift came out masked in its entirety.

"For ninety years they told us that covering your face was a lie you told about your own body. And then they covered theirs. So we thought — all right. We'll lie too. Let's all lie together and see who has the nerve to say it."
— Arco-2 lift technician

The police who stopped policing

By the sixth month the Directorate had detained more than nineteen thousand people against a capacity of about four thousand, releasing them within hours to make room; being arrested had stopped meaning anything. The institution split along a fault always in it — recruited from the lower terraces, officered from the upper — and junior personnel began refusing detention orders, several told me not on principle but on arithmetic. Everyone here dates the hinge precisely: at the Arco-1 concourse a Directorate line was ordered three times to clear a masked crowd with sonic disruptors and did not, and the senior officer — masked, and visibly afflicted himself — put up his weapon and walked off, and his line went with him. What followed was ugly. Stations burned in all three arcologies; at least four hundred died over six weeks, the great majority Health Police caught individually by crowds, a number of them the constables who had refused their orders. Nobody here defends it. Nobody is surprised by it.

What is there now

A provisional council of forty-one now sits in Arco-1 — accepted in the arcologies, contested in the agricultural belt, absent in orbit, where the Summersun Mercenary Co-operative still runs the starport because nobody has told it to stop. The statutes are suspended, not repealed, and the excellent medical infrastructure they justified stands largely unstaffed. Three factions matter: those wanting repeal; those wanting the statutes amended and kept, who argue — not without evidence — that a world this dense will have an epidemic within a decade without them; and the remnants of the Directorate, who keep their training and much of their equipment and are currently doing nothing at all, in a manner many here find more alarming than if they were doing something. Traffic is up sharply into a world with no customs regime and a shortage of almost everything. What is arriving is not, in the main, humanitarian.

The agitators who were not there

Throughout, the Directorate blamed offworld agitators. A persistent strand of opinion here — which I do not share — holds they were specific and named: the crew known from the popular broadcasts as the Heroes of Raschev. The entire basis is one recording, circulated by the Directorate in the Autarchy's final year, purporting to show one of them — the Aslan, Ora — assassinating the Autarch. It played for weeks. A warrant followed.

The recording is a fabrication — not my finding but the Directorate's own forensic section's, in an assessment dated eleven days after it first aired. Never published. The broadcast was never withdrawn, and the warrant never cancelled; at least one remains outstanding against a party the council cannot locate and appears disinclined to look for. When that surfaced during the collapse it did the Directorate more damage than the accusation had done to the accused. People did not conclude they had been mistaken, but that they had been managed — and probably about everything else.

So the theory can be dismissed, and I dismiss it. A fabricated proof is evidence of nothing but the character of whoever fabricated it. I add only that the fabrication was official, thorough, and aimed at precisely the question a historian would most like answered. Whether any offworlder had a hand in what happened here cannot now be settled either way, because the only evidence ever offered was invented by a government with every reason to invent it. That is not the same as an answer of no.

Method. 61 interviews; recovered Directorate detention logs; forensic assessments; council minutes; Highport traffic records. Not customs data, which I could not obtain. The origin of the condition remains unestablished. I would caution readers against assuming a small cause cannot have a large effect: a polity that has staked everything on a single claim about the world has a single point of failure, and what that failure turns out to be is rarely predictable and often, in retrospect, embarrassing.
