Shadows of the Poised State

A survey of systems adjacent to topological criticality

If the domain convergence hypothesis is real — if topology, criticality, autocatalysis, and computation truly meet at a single organizing principle — then we should find its shadows. Systems that don’t have all five features, but have enough of them to glow in the dark. Anomalous robustness where none should exist. Unexplained coherence. Suspiciously efficient self-organization.

This survey examines twelve candidate systems, scores them against the five-feature framework, and finds six cross-cutting patterns that suggest the shadows are real.

1. The Five-Feature Framework

The convergence hypothesis predicts co-occurrence of five features. Each is independently well-studied. Their co-occurrence is the signal.

☣ Criticality Power-law statistics, phase transitions, σ→1
◆ Topology Defects, protected states, winding numbers
↻ Autocatalysis Self-sustaining reaction networks, RAF closure
⚙ Computation Information processing at edge-of-chaos
✖ Braiding Non-trivial exchange, braid group statistics

A system with one or two features is interesting. A system with three or more is a candidate. A system where four or five converge without anyone having noticed the convergence — that’s a shadow of the poised state.

The Autocatalysis Gap

Across all twelve systems, autocatalytic closureA network of reactions where every catalyst needed is produced by some reaction within the network itself. Self-sustaining: given a food source, it maintains itself without external catalysts. Kauffman’s RAF sets formalize this. is consistently the weakest feature — present at best as structural analogy. If the poised-state hypothesis requires all five features to converge, the theory predicts that genuine autocatalytic closure should be discoverable in quasicrystal growth, turbulent cascade dynamics, and the 1/f noise mechanism. Its absence is the sharpest test of the framework.

4. Cardiac Tissue: Where KT Physics Meets Biology

A 2025 paper in Frontiers in Network Physiology made the connection explicit: atrial arrhythmias are topological states governed by Kosterlitz-Thouless physics. Sinus rhythm has no vortices. Atrial flutter = bound vortex-antivortex pairs. Atrial fibrillation = unbound vortices. The transition between arrhythmia types IS a topological phase transition.

This is cardiac tissue scoring strong on three convergence features simultaneously:

  • Criticality: Fibrillation onset shows critical slowing down, early warning signals
  • Topology: Phase singularities (spiral tips) with topological charge conservation; KT binding/unbinding
  • Autocatalysis: Ca2+-induced Ca2+ release (CICR) is explicitly autocatalytic

The Braid Question

Spiral wave tips trace trajectories in 2+1D spacetime. These trajectories braid. Has anyone computed the braid group representation of spiral tip dynamics during the flutter→fibrillation transition? If the braiding statistics change at the KT transition, cardiac tissue is a classical system exhibiting non-trivial exchange statistics — a shadow of anyonic physics in living matter.

5. Active Matter: Topological Defects That Build Bodies

In epithelial cell sheets, elongated cells form a nematic liquid crystalA material whose rod-shaped molecules align along a common direction but aren’t arranged in layers — orientational order without positional order. The alignment field can have topological defects: points where the orientation is undefined.. This living crystal has topological defectsPoints or lines where an order parameter field is undefined — singularities that cannot be removed by smooth deformation. Classified by their winding number (topological charge). Spiral wave tips, dislocations in crystals, and vortices in superfluids are all topological defects.: +1/2 defects (comets that self-propel) and −1/2 defects (trefoils that sit still). These defects aren’t incidental — they organize the tissue.

In 2022, Nature Materials showed that integer topological defects organize stresses driving tissue morphogenesis in regenerating Hydra. Defects mark where cells die, where tissues fold, where organs form. Topology tells biology where to build.

In 2025, Andersen et al. discovered an anti-hyperuniform critical state of topological defects in active nematics (Reports on Progress in Physics). Near a critical activity level, defect interactions become long-range, scaling with system size. Giant number fluctuations. The critical state IS defined by the defect statistics.

The Deepest Insight

The critical state and the topological order are not separate phenomena. They are aspects of the same thing. The critical state is what happens when topological defects reach a critical density. This is exactly what the convergence hypothesis predicts: criticality and topology co-emerge at the poised state.

6. Quasicrystals: The Fibonacci Bridge

Au51Al34Yb15 sits at a quantum critical point without external tuning (Deguchi et al., Nature Materials, 2012). No magnetic field, no pressure, no doping — the material is critical purely because of its aperiodic structure. Divergent susceptibility (χ−1 ~ T0.51), logarithmically divergent specific heat. The effect vanishes in crystalline approximants of near-identical composition. Structure alone drives criticality.

All electronic eigenstates in Fibonacci quasicrystals are critical — neither extended nor localized — with multifractalA fractal whose scaling behavior varies from point to point. Instead of one fractal dimension, there’s a continuous spectrum of local dimensions. Critical wavefunctions in quasicrystals are multifractal — neither fully extended nor fully localized. scaling. The energy spectrum is a self-similar Cantor set. A 2023 Nature Physics paper confirmed nested patterns of localized, delocalized, and multifractal states.

The Fibonacci Bridge

The golden ratio φ simultaneously governs:

  • Penrose tiling geometry (quasicrystal structure)
  • Fibonacci anyon fusion rules (topological quantum computation)
  • Multifractal critical wavefunctions (electronic criticality)

This is not numerology. The same algebraic structure underlies aperiodic order, topological quantum computation, and critical electronic states. The quasicrystal is where topology, criticality, and computation share a common mathematical root.

7. Glass Transition: A Hidden Bicritical System?

Pettini et al. (Entropy, 2025) found two specific heat peaks in Lennard-Jones glass formers: a second-order transition and a weakly first-order transition. Both are accompanied by topological changes in the potential energy landscape — inflection points in mean curvature, principal curvature dispersion, and scalar curvature variance, connected to topology by the Pinkall and Overholt theorems.

Simultaneously, the Gardner transition (glass → marginally stable subbasins) was shown in October 2024 to coincide with the emergence of jamming scalings. Two distinct ordering transitions with topological defects mediating both. If these interact at a codimension-twoA point in parameter space requiring two parameters to be tuned simultaneously. Like the intersection of two lines — one constraint gives a line, two constraints give a point. Bicritical points are codimension-two. point — that is a bicritical systemA system where two distinct phase transitions interact at a single point in parameter space. At a bicritical point, the universality class changes — the critical exponents are different from either transition considered alone..

Strategic Value

Condensed matter, same domain as MnF2. If the glass transition is bicritical, it can serve as the second validation system in the methods paper — one where the result is genuinely novel, not just reproducing known exponents.

8. Photosynthesis: Noise as Resource

The FMO complex in green sulfur bacteria transfers absorbed light energy to the reaction center with ~99% efficiency at room temperature. The mechanism: environment-assisted quantum transportA mechanism where environmental noise actually helps energy transfer instead of destroying it. At an optimal noise level, random fluctuations eliminate inefficient pathways while opening new channels. The biological analogue of stochastic resonance. (ENAQT). Noise doesn’t destroy quantum coherence — it helps. At the optimal dephasing strength, environmental fluctuations eliminate inefficient transport paths while opening new channels.

This pattern — noise as resource, not obstacle — appears across multiple poised-state candidates:

  • Avian compass: Thermal fluctuations enhance magnetosensitivity (stochastic resonance)
  • Enzyme catalysis: Protein thermal motions access tunneling-competent conformations
  • Brain: Optimal information processing at the critical point (not quiet, not noisy)
  • 1/f noise in dopant networks: Noise IS constitutive of computational capacity (2025)

The ENAQT Signature

In every case, the system sits at an exact point where environmental fluctuations maximize function. This is the hallmark shadow of poised-state operation. The system has organized itself to use noise rather than fight it.

9. Cross-System Patterns

Six patterns emerge across the twelve systems. Each is independently interesting. Their co-occurrence is the argument.

Pattern 1: Topological Defects as Organizing Principles

In active nematics, cardiac tissue, the glass transition, the brain, and the tokamak, topological defects aren’t incidental features. They organize the system. The cardiac KT paper makes it explicit: the transition between arrhythmia types IS a topological phase transition. Defect binding/unbinding = the thing that happens.

Systems: cardiac, active matter, glass, brain, tokamak, vortex knots

Pattern 2: Criticality and Topology Co-Emerge

The active nematic result is the clearest: the critical state IS defined by defect statistics becoming anti-hyperuniform. The critical state and topological order are not separate phenomena — they’re aspects of the same thing. This is exactly what the convergence hypothesis predicts.

Systems: active matter, glass transition, brain, quasicrystals

Pattern 3: Noise as Resource (ENAQT Pattern)

The system uses noise, not fights it. FMO: optimal transport at specific dephasing. Avian compass: quantum Zeno protects coherence while thermal noise enhances sensitivity. Enzyme catalysis: thermal fluctuations access tunneling conformations. Brain: optimal information at the critical point. 1/f noise in dopant networks: noise IS computational capacity.

Systems: FMO, avian compass, enzyme, brain, 1/f noise

Pattern 4: Helicity and Chirality Conservation

Topological invariants are conserved across scale transitions. Vortex knots: helicity flows from knotting to coiling (large→small scale) during reconnection. Avian compass: chirality-bolstered Zeno effect. Protein folding: topological knots constrain pathways. The system preserves topological information even as it transforms.

Systems: vortex knots, avian compass, protein folding, quasicrystals

Pattern 5: Multi-Critical Convergence

Protein folding simultaneously touches four phase-transition frameworks: thermodynamic critical point, jamming transition (φc = 0.55, δ = 9/2), SOC (fractal scaling), and edge-of-chaos (IDPs). That four usually-separate phenomena converge in one system is either coincidence or evidence that biology preferentially occupies multi-critical points.

Systems: protein folding (4 frameworks), glass transition (2), cardiac (3)

Pattern 6: Three-Regime Universal Structure

Almost every system has: ordered → critical → disordered. The poised state operates in the middle. Active nematics: ordered → anti-hyperuniform → turbulent. Cardiac: sinus → flutter → fibrillation. Brain: subcritical → critical → seizure. Tokamak: L-mode → dithering → H-mode.

Systems: active matter, cardiac, brain, tokamak, glass, quasicrystals

10. The KT Hypothesis

The Kosterlitz-ThoulessA phase transition driven by the unbinding of topological defect pairs. Below the transition, defects are bound in neutral pairs. Above it, they fly apart and disorder the system. Nobel Prize 2016. transition is the unbinding of topological defect pairs. It was discovered in 2D superfluids (Nobel Prize, 2016). It is now appearing in:

  • Cardiac tissue (Frontiers, 2025) — AFL = bound pairs, AF = unbound
  • Active nematics (Reports on Progress in Physics, 2025) — defect binding at critical activity
  • Potentially the glass transition — if two transitions involve defect binding
  • The original contexts: 2D superfluids, superconductors, XY magnets

The KT transition is:

  1. A phase transition (criticality)
  2. Mediated by topological defects (topology)
  3. Universal (independent of microscopic details)
  4. Native to 2D systems (relevant to 2DEG physics)

If the convergence hypothesis predicts a universal mechanism linking criticality and topology, the KT transition is the leading candidate. Wherever you find a KT-like transition, you are near a poised state.

11. Falsifiable Predictions

1. Glass transition bicriticality. Apply the five-point framework to glass-forming systems. If the Gardner transition and glass transition interact at a codimension-two point with universality class change, that’s a methods paper result. The topological markers identified by Pettini et al. provide the diagnostic.
2. Cardiac KT exponents. Measure fibrillation onset statistics — specifically the correlation length divergence and specific heat — and compare to KT universality predictions. If they match, cardiac tissue is a poised-state system with the same topological physics as superfluid helium.
3. Active matter defect braiding. Track nematic defect trajectories in 2+1D and compute braid group representations. Non-trivial braiding = anyonic statistics in a classical system. This would be the first demonstration of effective anyonic physics in living matter.
4. Quasicrystal Fibonacci bridge. The multifractal spectrum f(α) of electronic wavefunctions in Au-Al-Yb at the QCP should match Fibonacci model predictions. Information injected into phason degrees of freedom should propagate rather than dissipate. Vortices in quasicrystalline superconductors should exhibit statistics distinct from periodic superconductors.
5. Protein folding universality. The anomalous jamming exponent δ = 9/2 (unique universality class) should be universal across fold types. Mutations preserving knot invariant should maintain function; those altering knot type should be catastrophic even with preserved local structure.
6. Autocatalytic closure discovery. Genuine autocatalytic closure should be discoverable in quasicrystal growth, turbulent cascade dynamics, and the 1/f noise mechanism. Its current absence is the sharpest test: find it, and the framework gains a powerful confirmation; fail to find it after thorough search, and the five-feature model needs revision.
7. Neural topology × criticality. Connectome topology (Betti numbers, persistent homology) should predict critical exponents of neural avalanches. Topological structure determines the universality class of the critical state.
