With 1 state, agents can only play unconditionally. Always Defect dominates -- cooperation hovers near zero.

With 2 states, conditional strategies like Tit-for-Tat become possible. Cooperation emerges through reciprocity.

The transition is sharp -- a single additional state, the minimum unit of computational capacity, produces a qualitative change in social behavior. This is Miller's central finding: social behavior requires crossing a computational threshold.
