§1 — what this is

A short read of the toolkit, from someone validating and extending it

Three things, in order of how much you should trust them. (a) I reproduced your published chick-heart results — above. (b) An honest calibration of where our univariate eigenvalue methods stand on single period-doubling: they are worse than your deep-learning classifier and the standard variance / lag-1 AC baselines, and I say so plainly. (c) The direction I'm taking the method — a multivariate eigenvalue indicator aimed at discordant alternans, whose anti-phase mode a symmetric observable is structurally blind to (in the clean-swap limit). That last part is simulation so far, and it is labelled as such everywhere it appears.

▸prior work — the lineage this extendsCritical slowing down, the S-map eigenvalue, and fifteen years of spatial EWS. This is a mode-resolved reading of an established idea, not a new one.

The mechanism is critical slowing down. As a bifurcation approaches, the leading eigenvalue of the linearised return map moves to the stability boundary — toward +1 at a fold, toward −1 at a flip (period-doubling); recovery from perturbations slows, and variance and lag-1 autocorrelation rise (van Nes & Scheffer 2007; Scheffer et al. 2009; Dakos et al. 2012).

The dynamical eigenvalue (DEV) reads that eigenvalue directly: it is the dominant eigenvalue of the local S-map Jacobian (Sugihara 1994) estimated over a delay embedding (Takens 1981), used as an early-warning indicator by Grziwotz et al. 2023. The univariate cardiac-eigenvalue baseline I run alongside it — DE-AC (Ma & Zeng 2026) — extracts the same eigenvalue from the autocorrelation. The multivariate interaction Jacobian whose spectrum the multivariate estimator reads is the multivariate S-map of Deyle et al. 2016; the move to the eigenvector of the slowest-recovering mode in multivariate systems was developed in ecology (Weinans et al. 2019).

Spatial EWS is a mature subfield — spatial variance and skewness (Guttal & Jayaprakash 2009), spatial correlation (Dakos et al. 2010), spatial-pattern methods (Kéfi et al. 2014), and a maintained toolkit (spatialwarnings, Génin et al. 2018). The contribution here is not the claim that spatial information helps — that is established — but a mode-resolved eigenvalue observable that isolates the anti-phase mode a symmetric readout cannot.

The limitation, stated plainly

Our λ-estimators are data-hungry on short records — that's the whole story on single-PD, and it's a real limitation. An eigenvalue method does not earn its keep here. Its only single-PD edge is interpretability: the eigenvalue trajectory λ → −1 types the period-doubling mode mechanistically — a second, model-based route to the bifurcation type your DL classifier already predicts statistically (Bury et al. 2021, 2023). Variance alone does not type the mode; the eigenvalue and the DL classifier are complementary routes to it, not rivals. On detection performance, yours is better. We say so.

§4 — the direction

The mode a single observable can't see

Discordant alternans — adjacent tissue regions alternating out of phase, a well-established route to reentry and VF (Weiss et al. 2006) — carries an anti-phase spatial mode. In the idealized clean-swap limit that mode is odd under the spatial swap symmetry: it projects to zero on any symmetric observable, so a spatial mean sees none of it. (Real geometry breaks the exact symmetry — which is precisely why microvolt T-wave alternans is a validated body-surface predictor of arrhythmic risk (Rosenbaum et al. 1994); the aim here is to recover the magnitude of that broken-symmetry mode more directly.) The antisymmetric observable itself (the difference x₁−x₂) is fully sensitive to the mode — so going multivariate is not required for detectability in principle; its value is that multivariate DEV resolves the mode automatically, without knowing the right projection in advance. The joint multivariate Jacobian carries it, and its anti-phase branch of the spectrum slides to −1 as discordance approaches.

▸the math — why the mean is blind

Take two sites, state (x₁, x₂), and the swap S: (x₁,x₂) ↦ (x₂,x₁). Its eigenvectors split the space:

e₊ = (1, 1)/√2   — symmetric, S e₊ = +e₊
e₋ = (1, −1)/√2   — antisymmetric, S e₋ = −e₋

The spatial mean is exactly the projection onto the symmetric axis: ½(x₁+x₂) ∝ ⟨e₊, x⟩. A pure anti-phase perturbation lives on e₋, so ⟨e₊, e₋⟩ = 0 — its contribution to any symmetric readout is identically zero. A single lead that happens to weight the sites unequally sees a little of it, but the mean (and any symmetric observable) sees none, for every noise level and every gain.

▸proof sketch — two modes, one crosses −1

Linearise the beat-to-beat restitution map of the coupled two-cell system about its 1:1 fixed point. Coupling makes the Jacobian symmetric under S, so it block-diagonalises on e₊ / e₋: an in-phase (concordant) eigenvalue and an anti-phase (discordant) eigenvalue. Alternans is a period-doubling: some eigenvalue passes through −1. In this idealized coupled two-cell map the anti-phase (odd) branch is the one that can reach −1 — so it is the mode a symmetric observable is blind to. This is a symmetry demonstration, not tissue physiology: real electrotonic (diffusive) coupling synchronizes neighbours toward concordance, and genuine discordant alternans is a spatially-extended phenomenon set by CV-restitution × APD-restitution over a wavelength — the unstable eigenmode of the linearized cable (Echebarria & Karma 2002), not of a 2-cell Jacobian. The physiological discordant-alternans test therefore lives in the cable / CV-restitution model (§5), which this two-cell result motivates but does not stand in for. A scalar EWS built on a symmetric observable can only estimate the e₊ eigenvalue; the multivariate DEV estimates the whole 2×2 spectrum and reports the e₋ branch as it goes critical.

Symmetry-obstruction proof-of-concept — not tissue

Read this as a simulation result

Two separate claims. Analytic (0 runs needed): under exact swap symmetry a symmetric observable — the spatial mean — projects the anti-phase mode to identically zero, so it is blind by construction (proof above). Empirical: in a coupled two-cell restitution model (15 runs — three noise levels, SD ∈ {0.5, 1.0, 2.0} ms, five seeds each) the multivariate DEV estimator resolved the anti-phase eigenvalue → −1 (dominant Re λ ≤ −0.9) in all 15 runs, while every mean-observable single-eigenvalue method — univariate DEV, and DE-AC on the mean — resolved it in none (0 / 15). The separation is categorical, not a lead-time; two independent λ-estimators agree. The obstruction is a property of the observable, not of any one indicator: any symmetric readout (mean, variance, lag-1 AC, or an eigenvalue estimated from them) is odd-mode blind. This is a controlled model, not tissue. It demonstrates the obstruction is real and the multivariate indicator clears it; it does not establish anticipation on real hearts. That's §5.

▸details — and the honest boundary for real tissue

The sim: two restitution cells, diffusive coupling, additive noise swept across levels; pre-registered pass/fail on "does the anti-phase eigenvalue reach −1 before the mean-observable method flags anything." Two independent λ-estimators (the S-map Jacobian eigenvalue and the DE-AC autocorrelation form) to guard against estimator-specific artefacts. S-map hyperparameters — embedding dimension E, localization weight θ, and Tikhonov ridge — were fixed a priori across the noise sweep, not tuned by forecast cross-validation, so the estimator is configured for coefficient inference rather than forecast skill (cf. Cenci et al. 2019, best-forecast ≠ best-inference).

The boundary — and I want to be precise here, because it matters: real discordant patterns have a moving nodal line, not a clean 2-region swap. So a spatial mean is only approximately blind (the cancellation isn't exact once the nodal line drifts), and a single pixel is not blind at all — it sees its own local phase. The claim is not "single observables see nothing." The claim is that multivariate DEV resolves the multiple modes and flags the anti-phase eigenvalue earlier than a single-site or mean method structurally can. That is an empirical anticipation test on real tissue — and I've now run the first attempt (§5): the staging is faithful, but this recording can't resolve the discordant regime, so the definitive test still needs the right data.

§5 — the plan

Real optical-mapping tissue first, on our own — then, maybe, your clinical data

We sourced public, spatially-resolved cardiac optical-mapping data with decrementing-pacing ramps toward VF (O'Shea 2022: guinea-pig whole-heart, 64×64 at 500 Hz) and ran the first real-tissue test ourselves — two honest results. (1) The staging is faithful: our pipeline reproduces O'Shea's own APD maps (APD80 106 ms vs their 108, ΔAPD80 10.0 ms vs 10.2, restitution tracking their Fig 2b) — so when it flags something, it isn't a staging artefact. (2) But this recording can't resolve the discordant regime: APD becomes unmeasurable near VF — conduction block and wavebreak drop per-beat pixel validity to ~0.3 — so genuine discordant alternans can't be tracked where it matters. (Guinea-pig is also a comparatively weak discordant-alternans model — relatively flat APD restitution; the canonical DA literature is rabbit and canine — which deepens the data-adequacy limit rather than pointing at the method.) A pre-registered data-inadequate null, honestly a limit of the data, not a result about the method. So the definitive anti-phase test needs data with clean APD through the discordant regime (higher-SNR optical mapping, or a protocol that dwells in sustained discordance before block); meanwhile the mechanism is already demonstrated in the controlled two-cell simulation above, which I'm extending to a spatially-extended cable model with a known anti-phase mode.

If it pans out, the multivariate indicator could sit naturally alongside ewstools' existing indicators — a spatially-resolved, interpretable eigenvalue EWS complementing the DL classifier — but that is a decision for you as maintainer, not an assumption on my part. Complement, not competitor: yours scales and classifies; the eigenvalue is mechanistic and multi-mode.

Where your input would matter most

The controlled simulation is our proof-of-mechanism; the animal optical maps validated our staging but showed that public ramp-to-VF data can't carry the anti-phase test. The data that would matter most is yours — or clinical: spatially-resolved human alternans with clean APD through discordance. That's the conversation I'd like to have Tuesday.

§6 — the boundary

What I'm not claiming

  • Not superiority on single-PD. You're better — §3. Our univariate eigenvalue methods are behind your variance and DL, and the reason (short-record data hunger) is a real limitation.
  • Not that the multivariate result is established on tissue. It's simulation so far — §4. A controlled two-cell model, pre-registered, not real hearts.
  • Not that I invented multivariate dynamical eigenvalues. The multivariate interaction Jacobian this rests on is Deyle et al. 2016 (the multivariate S-map); reading its dominant eigenvalue as an early-warning signal is Grziwotz et al. 2023; and the whole construct is a mode-resolved reading of critical slowing down (van Nes & Scheffer 2007). The move to a slowest-recovering-mode eigenvector in multivariate systems was developed in ecology (Weinans et al. 2019).
what I am pursuing

A multivariate DEV that resolves the anti-phase discordant mode and anticipates it on real cardiac tissue — interpretably, and benchmarked against your own methods.
