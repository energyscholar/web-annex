Content-Selective Search De-Indexing: A Reproducibility Report

Bruce Stephenson · June 2026 · Initial measurement: June 21, 2026
Abstract

We document content-selective search de-indexing across three platforms (Cryptome, Slashdot, Substack). Control cases on each platform demonstrate that the de-indexing is content-based, not domain-level or SEO-related. Every test in this report is a Google search that a reader can perform independently in under five minutes. We make no claims about the mechanism or intent behind the de-indexing. This report documents that it occurs, provides evidence of selectivity, and is designed for independent replication.

1. Methodology

What We Test

Whether specific URLs that exist and return HTTP 200 appear in Google search results when searched by exact title, by content keywords, or by the site: operator.

What Constitutes a Control

Content on the same domain, by the same author, with similar age and traffic characteristics, that is indexed by Google. If the suppressed content's absence were caused by domain-level blocking, poor SEO, or low traffic, the control content would be equally absent.

Tool Required

Google Search. No specialized software, subscriptions, or technical skills are needed.

Selection Criteria

Only cases that meet all three conditions are included:

  • Replicable by anyone in 30 seconds with a Google search
  • Have a control case on the same platform proving selectivity is not domain-level
  • Cannot be explained by any standard alternative (low traffic, robots.txt, platform moderation, content age, noindex tags)

Cases that are ambiguous, require platform-specific knowledge, or could plausibly be explained by organic search behavior are excluded.

Temporal Note

These findings may be transient

In at least one prior case on a third platform, content-selective de-indexing was lifted shortly after an independent technical authority examined and confirmed the pattern. The de-indexed content had been invisible to search engines for over a decade; within weeks of independent verification, it became findable again.

This suggests the de-indexing state may be actively maintained and responsive to external scrutiny. Researchers are advised to document their initial findings immediately upon replication — including screenshots and timestamps — as the observable state may change once verification is underway.

3. Controls & Alternative Explanations

Each standard explanation for a URL not appearing in search results is examined and ruled out.

Alternative Explanation Evidence Against
Domain-level blocking C01 proves postquantum.substack.com is indexed. C02 proves Slashdot comments by the same user are indexed (10+). Other Cryptome pages (e.g., the site's front page, its widely linked documents) appear normally in Google results. The de-indexing is page-specific, not domain-level.
Low PageRank / poor SEO T02, T03, and C01 are on the same Substack account by the same author, with similar publication dates and audience. Substack controls the page structure, metadata, and SEO for all articles equally. T01 (Cryptome) has 42 Wayback Machine captures over 12 years — Google's crawler has visited it hundreds of times. T04 (Slashdot) is a Score 5 comment by a user whose other 10+ comments are all indexed (C02).
robots.txt exclusion Substack's robots.txt does not disallow post pages. All articles from the account (including C01) use the same URL structure (/p/slug).
Cryptome's robots.txt does not disallow the path /2012/03/qc-footprint.htm.
Slashdot's robots.txt does not disallow comment pages — and C02 proves they are indexed when not selectively excluded.
noindex meta tag Substack controls the <meta> tags for all articles. Since C01 is indexed, Substack does not set noindex on articles by default. The suppressed and control articles use the same template. T01 (Cryptome) uses static HTML with no noindex directive. T04 (Slashdot) uses the same comment template as the 10+ indexed comments by the same user.
Content age T01 was published in March 2012. T04 is from June 2012. T02 and T03 are from 2022–2024. C01 is from December 2022. C02 includes comments from 2011–2013, the same era as T04. Age does not correlate with de-indexing — old and recent content are both affected, while content of similar age is indexed.
Platform moderation All URLs return HTTP 200 — no platform has removed or restricted the content. The content is publicly accessible to anyone who visits the URL directly.

5. Reproducibility Instructions

Replicate the Full Battery

  1. Open Google Search in a standard browser (private/incognito window recommended to avoid personalization).
  2. For each test above, click "Copy query" and paste it into Google, or click "Try it on Google."
  3. Record whether the target URL appears anywhere in the results.
  4. Click each "Direct link" to confirm the content still exists (HTTP 200).

Test from Different Locations

  • Try Google domains for different countries: google.co.uk, google.de, google.ca
  • Use a VPN to test from different geographic regions
  • Results that differ by location are themselves informative — note the location in your log

Test on Alternative Search Engines

  • Bing — repeat each query, note whether suppression is Google-specific
  • DuckDuckGo — uses Bing's index; differences from Bing indicate DDG-specific filtering
  • Brave Search — independent index

Set Up Monitoring

To track changes over time, re-run the full battery monthly and record the date and result for each test. Any change in status (SUPPRESSED → INDEXED or vice versa) is itself a data point worth documenting.
