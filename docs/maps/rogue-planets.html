Background

The spinward edge of District 268 has a navigational gap. The main trade corridor runs through Walston–Flexos–Collace (hexes 1232–1237), but reaching the spinward systems (Squallia 1133, Faldor 1131, Bowman 1132) requires Jump-2 minimum. Jump-1 ships are locked out. This chokes commerce, limits patrol range, and keeps the spinward systems economically marginal.

Two rogue planets — gravitationally unbound from any star — have been identified in this gap. They offer potential deep space refueling waypoints that could open the route to Jump-1 traffic.

What are rogue planets? Planets ejected from their parent star systems during formation, or formed independently from gas cloud collapse. Current astrophysical estimates suggest 1–10 rogue planets per star in the galaxy. They are difficult to detect without deliberate infrared surveys, which is why these were not catalogued until recently.

Erebus — Rogue Gas Giant (Hex 1235)

Physical Profile

TypeSmall gas giant (Saturn analogue)
Mass~0.3 Jupiter masses
Radius~55,000 km
Atmosphere96% H₂, 3% He, trace CH₄/NH₃
Cloud-top Temperature~60K (internal heat only — no stellar radiation)
VisibilityFaint infrared glow from gravitational contraction. Invisible to optical sensors at interstellar range.
Ring SystemIce and rock debris ring. Visible only on radar/IR.
Moons3–4 detected. One icy body large enough for surface operations.
Parent StarNone. Gravitationally unbound.

Fuel Extraction

Method: Standard atmospheric skimming. Hydrogen is the primary atmospheric component. Conventional fuel scoops are effective.

Supply: Functionally unlimited. Equivalent to any inhabited-system gas giant.

Advantage: Same-day refueling. No infrastructure required beyond the ship's own scoops.

Hazards CREW ADVISORY

  • Total darkness. No star. All operations by sensor, radar, and floodlight. Cloud layers are black. The ring system is invisible to the naked eye. Pilots report spatial disorientation and vertigo.
  • Radiation belts. Saturn-class magnetosphere with trapped charged particles. Skimming requires specific entry corridors through the magnetotail. Unshielded exposure is lethal within hours. Monitor radiation dosimeters continuously.
  • Atmospheric turbulence. Without stellar heating, weather is driven by internal heat alone. Storm cells are deep, slow, and unpredictable. Downdrafts can drag a ship into the high-pressure interior with little warning.
  • Ring particle impacts. Fuel skimming orbits may intersect ring debris trajectories. Micro-impacts at orbital velocity. Maintain active sensor watch and plot approach vectors to minimize ring-plane crossings.
Operational summary: Erebus is a straightforward refueling stop — mechanically identical to any gas giant. The hazards are environmental, not exotic. The difficulty is the darkness: every procedure that normally relies on visual reference must be done on instruments. Crew should expect elevated stress during approach and skimming operations.

Nerthus — Rogue Super-Earth (Hex 1134)

Physical Profile

TypeSuper-Earth with global ice shell over subsurface liquid ocean
Mass~3 Earth masses
Radius~9,500 km
Surface40–80 km water ice. Surface temperature ~35K.
Subsurface Ocean~200 km deep liquid water. 275–280K at the rocky seafloor. Heated by radioactive decay (U-238, Th-232, K-40).
AtmosphereNitrogen/argon, ~0.01 atm. Too thin to breathe. Enough to carry sound faintly.
Hydrothermal VentsDetected at ocean floor via thermal mapping (1082 survey). Iron-sulfur chemistry, alkaline pH.
MoonsNone detected.
Parent StarNone. Gravitationally unbound.

Fuel Extraction

Method: Two-stage process.

  1. Mine surface ice (trivially abundant — the entire planet is covered)
  2. Melt → electrolyze → liquid hydrogen

Supply: Functionally infinite. The ice sheet is 40–80 km thick across the entire surface.

Requirement: Surface installation with power plant and electrolysis unit. A TL-12+ prefab station could be operational within weeks. Not instant like gas giant skimming — but no tanker dependency.

Advantage: Once operational, eliminates tanker logistics entirely. The Nerthus–Squallia link (1 hex, J1) opens the spinward route to all traffic.

Hazards CREW ADVISORY

  • Total darkness. Same as Erebus — no parent star, no ambient light. Surface operations by floodlight only.
  • Cryovolcanism. The ice shell is geologically active. Geysers of liquid water and ammonia erupt without warning — plumes reaching 50+ km altitude. Surface bases near fissure zones are at risk of being hit, buried, or toppled.
  • Ice quakes. Tidal stresses from uneven radiogenic heating cause the ice shell to shift. Crevasses open without warning. Equipment can fall into melt-pockets.
  • Subsurface instability. The ice is not solid bedrock. Thermal gradients create weak zones. Load-bearing assessments required before establishing any permanent structure.

Scientific Interest XENOBIOLOGY

The 1082 IISS survey detected hydrothermal vent activity at the ocean floor. Thermal mapping indicates an active geochemical system consistent with chemosynthetic biology — life sustained by chemical energy rather than sunlight.

No direct biological sampling has been conducted. The 1118 mission was intended to perform subsurface surveys but was lost before completing its objectives.

If confirmed, Nerthus would represent a biosphere that has existed for billions of years without ever seeing a star. The scientific implications are significant.

Operational summary: Nerthus requires more investment than Erebus — surface infrastructure for ice mining vs. simple atmospheric skimming. But it is the higher-value target: the J1 link to Squallia makes it the commerce breakthrough. The scientific potential of the subsurface ocean adds long-term value. The missing 1118 mission is an unresolved concern.

Strategic Assessment

Erebus

  • Immediate refueling capability
  • No infrastructure investment needed
  • J2 from Collace — accessible now
  • Environmental hazards are known and manageable
  • Enables Collace–Squallia at J2

Nerthus

  • Requires surface installation (weeks)
  • Higher investment, higher payoff
  • J1 to Squallia — opens route to all ships
  • Scientific value (potential biosphere)
  • Unresolved safety concern (missing mission)

Whoever controls these refueling points controls the spinward route through District 268. Collace, the Imperium, Trexalon, and independent operators all have reason to claim them.
