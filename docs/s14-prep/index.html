THE CLIFFHANGER. Show Arabus system map. Read this. End session.

Read Aloud — Session Closer
The jump bubble collapses. Stars return — but one of them is wrong. Low on the viewscreen, below the familiar constellations that Von Sydo uses for orientation, there's a point of light that shouldn't be there. It's faint, but it's not a star. Stars are white, or blue, or yellow. This one is red. Cherry red. The color of cooling iron.

"Contact," says Jeri from the sensor station. "Bearing zero-one-five, mark minus twelve. Range... working." A pause. "Range four hundred thousand kilometers. Massive gravitational return. That's not a planet. That's almost a star."

On the main display, the enhanced sensors paint their first picture of Arabus. A dark sphere, self-glowing, banded with currents of dull heat. No sun illuminates it. It makes its own light. And it fills more sky than anything has a right to, out here in the absolute black between stars.

Max, at the engineering station, reads the numbers as they come in. Eight Jupiter masses. Cloud-top temperature four hundred and fifty Kelvin. Magnetic field... enormous. Extending in every direction, uncompressed, unconfined.

"There's a moon," Jeri adds. "Active. Volcanic. In close orbit."

The cherry-red light fills the bridge. No one speaks for a moment.

END SESSION. Do not add anything after the read-aloud.
