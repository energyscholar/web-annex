Ashkin–Teller for the Working Mathematician

Two Ising copies, one marginal coupling, a line of critical points

Take two copies of the critical Ising model that ignore each other, then couple them with one term of exactly the right kind: the product of their two energy densities. The critical point does not move or split. It becomes one end of a whole line of critical points, along which some exponents slide continuously while others stay pinned. This is the Ashkin–Teller model.[7] The page builds it from the double Ising model up, with every number sourced. It matters to anyone who reads exponents or fluctuation indicators off data: on this line the order parameter’s variance scales identically everywhere, the energy’s does not, and the textbook signature of an abrupt transition appears at a continuous one.

1One Ising model, in five lines

Spins σi = ±1 on the square lattice, H = −K Σ⟨ij⟩ σiσj, with β absorbed into K. Kramers–Wannier duality maps the model at coupling K onto itself at a dual coupling; on the square lattice the self-dualLeft unchanged by the duality map. If the model has a single transition, it must sit on the self-dual set. point is sinh 2K = 1,[2] so Kc = ½ ln(1 + √2) = 0.44069.[1] The exponents, read off the formulas of §4 at the Ising end: ν = 1, α/ν = 0, γ/ν = 7/4, η = 1/4. In scaling dimensionsx: at criticality a local operator’s two-point function decays as r−2x. A coupling h to that operator scales as b2−x under coarse-graining by b (in two dimensions)., the spin has xσ = η/2 = 1/8 and the energy density xε = 2 − 1/ν = 1. Already here a power-law fit misleads: from the specific heat Wiseman and Domany obtain α/ν = 0.23(1) with a log–log fit and 0 with a semi-log fit.[1]

2Two copies: the double Ising model

Put a second Ising spin τi on every site, and give each copy its own coupling K = Kc with no term between them. The partition function factorises, Z = ZIsing(K)2. The symmetry is Z2 × Z2 (flip every σ; flip every τ; independently), and there are three Z2 order parameters: σ, τ and the product στ, which survives flipping both. Independence gives the product’s correlations for free:

⟨σ0τ0 σrτr⟩ = ⟨σ0σr⟩⟨τ0τr⟩ ~ r−1/2,   so xστ = 1/4.

3Turn on the coupling

The Ashkin–Teller Hamiltonian adds the four-spin term, with β absorbed:[1]

H = −Σ⟨ij⟩ [ K σiσj + K τiτj + L σiσjτiτj ].

The new term is the product of the two copies’ bond energies, (σiσj)(τiτj). At the decoupled point it couples to εσετ: x = 2, y = 0, marginal. Marginal to linear order is not enough for a line, since a marginal coupling can still grow or die slowly at the next order. This one does neither: it is exactly marginal, and Ginsparg identifies ε1ε2 as the operator that moves the theory along a line of critical theories.[3] Turning L on therefore does not take the pair off criticality; it slides it along a line, and the exponents slide with it. At L = K the four states (σ, τ) = (±1, ±1) are all alike and the model is the four-state Potts modelq states per site; neighbours gain energy only when they are in the same state. q = 2 is Ising., where σ, τ and στ are interchangeable.[1]

The contrast is the on-site bilinear coupling g Σi σiτi. It multiplies στ, with x = 1/4 and y = 7/4: strongly relevant. The decoupled point is unstable in the g direction and there is no line. Where that flow ends is expected, not shown here.

Why ε1ε2 is exactly marginal

Ginsparg’s criterion: an operator V of dimension (1,1) generates a one-parameter family of critical theories only if, to leading order, the V × V operator product contains no term in V itself. If it does, V’s dimension shifts once the coupling is on, and V is marginally relevant on one side and marginally irrelevant on the other.[3]

For Ashkin–Teller the check passes for a structural reason. On the critical line, ε1ε2 is the four-fermion term of the massless Thirring model, which only changes the radius of a free boson. Changing a radius is a motion along a line, so no fixed point is left.[3] The bilinear σ1σ2 has no such protection; with y = 7/4 the question of higher orders never arises.

4The phase diagram

In Wiseman and Domany’s coordinates the phase diagram is the unit square X, Z ∈ [0, 1]:[1]

Z = e−4K,   X = e−2(K+L);    K = −¼ ln Z,   L = −½ ln X − K.

The parabola Z = X2 is L = 0 (two decoupled Ising models); the diagonal Z = X is L = K (four-state Potts). Duality fixes the critical line exactly on the self-dual line Z = 1 − 2X, equivalently e−2L = sinh 2K. Its segment from X0 = (√2 − 1, 3 − 2√2), the decoupled Ising point (K = 0.44069, L = 0), to X4 = (1/3, 1/3), the Potts point (K = L = ¼ ln 3 = 0.27465), is a line of critical points with continuously varying exponents. Below X0 (L < 0) the same line continues as the ferro–para boundary. Three phases: P, paramagnetic; F, ferromagnetic, with ⟨σ⟩ = ±⟨τ⟩ and ⟨στ⟩ ≠ 0; and a ⟨στ⟩ phase in which only the product orders (⟨σ⟩ = ⟨τ⟩ = 0), which exists only for L > K. Two further boundaries leave X4 and flow to Ising fixed points; they have no exact equation and are not drawn.[1]

Along the segment the exponents come from the Coulomb gasA standard exact method for 2D critical models (Nienhuis). Its single coupling, here gR, labels the position on the line and fixes the exponents., as quoted by Wiseman and Domany:[1]

gR = (8/π) arcsin[½ coth 2K],   yt = 2 − 2/gR,   α/ν = 2yt − 2,   γ/ν = 7/4.

At X0, ½ coth 2K = 1/√2 and gR = 2; at X4 it is 1 and gR = 4. By algebra, ν = 1/yt runs from 1 to 2/3, α/ν from 0 to 1, and the thermal dimension xε = 2 − yt from 1 to 1/2, while η = 2 − γ/ν = 1/4 never moves. At L = 0 this is sinh 2K = 1, the Ising self-dual point of §1. On the line, coth 2K = (1 + Z)/(1 − Z) = (1 − X)/X, which is how the readout computes gR from X alone. The point X0 is where the line meets the parabola: 1 − 2X = X2 gives X = √2 − 1; X4 is where it meets the diagonal, X = 1/3.[1]

5Live Monte Carlo: two layers on the line

The simulation uses Wiseman and Domany’s cluster method.[1] Freeze τ. Then σ alone is an Ising model with bond couplings Jjk = K + L τjτk, ferromagnetic whenever L ≤ K. Grow one WolffWolff and Swendsen–Wang are cluster algorithms for Ising-type models: instead of single spins, they flip whole clusters of aligned spins at once. cluster on σ and flip it; then swap the roles of σ and τ. At L = 0 and at L = K this reduces to the standard Wolff and Swendsen–Wang methods. The controls are the position t on the critical segment and a temperature factor f that sends (K, L) to (K/f, L/f): f = 1 is on the line, f < 1 colder and ordered, f > 1 hotter and disordered. Dividing both couplings by f keeps L/K fixed, so L ≤ K holds everywhere the sliders reach. One sweep is enough cluster flips to turn over about ℓ2 spins.

6Weak universality: what slides and what is pinned

Suzuki’s weak universality, as Khan et al. state it: along a line generated by a marginal parameter, β, γ and ν vary, but β/ν and γ/ν, and hence δ and η, stay fixed.[4] Ashkin–Teller obeys it: ν slides from 1 to 2/3, so γ = (7/4)ν slides with it, while γ/ν = 7/4 and η = 1/4 are pinned along the whole segment.[1]

Khan et al. report a magnet that does not: in (Sm1−yNdy)0.52Sr0.48MnO3 (y is the Nd fraction here, not an RG eigenvalue), β, γ and δ all vary with composition, δ included.[4]

7For the indicator-builder

Four readings of the facts above. Each is a statement about this model and these measurements; the page cites no early-warning literature and makes no claim about it.

(a) The order parameter’s variance cannot tell you where you are. χ = ℓ2⟨M2⟩ scales as ℓ7/4 at every point of the segment, with fitted γ/ν between 1.751 and 1.763 at all five points. The energy variance, C ~ ℓα/ν, has an exponent running from 0 to 1. For an indicator this means a variance-type signal built on the order parameter is blind to position along a marginal line, while an energy-fluctuation signal is not.[1]

(b) Exponent fits at accessible sizes are biased, and at the Potts end badly. At X1 the fit gives 0.38(2) against an exact 0.2096; at X4, 0.747(3) against 1. At X4 the specific heat carries a multiplicative logarithmA logarithmic factor multiplying the leading power law, here C ~ ℓ/(ln ℓ)3/2, rather than a separate correction term added to it., C ~ ℓ/(ln ℓ)3/2,[5] so a power-law fit returns α/ν well below 1. The corresponding correction expected in χ, ℓ7/4(ln ℓ)−1/8, is not visible for ℓ ≤ 256.[6] For an indicator this means the quoted errors (statistical only) do not cover the systematic error, which is largest at the Potts end.

(c) Bimodality does not certify an abrupt transition. At the four-state Potts point, and on the Ashkin–Teller line at L/K = 0.95, both known to be continuous, Jin, Sen and Sandvik find double-peaked energy and order-parameter histograms near the transition and a negative minimum of the Binder cumulantU = 2[1 − ⟨m4⟩/(2⟨m2⟩2)] in Jin, Sen and Sandvik’s normalisation. A negative minimum, together with double-peaked histograms, is what they call pseudo-first-order behaviour. U = 2[1 − ⟨m4⟩/(2⟨m2⟩2)], for sizes up to ℓ = 256.[6] They call it pseudo-first-order. For an indicator this means a bimodal distribution near a transition, on its own, does not show the transition is discontinuous.

(d) Slowing down: what is sourced is an algorithm’s, not the model’s. The only dynamic exponents in hand are those of Wiseman and Domany’s cluster algorithm: zint,χ, the exponent of the integrated autocorrelation time of χ (last column of the table in §4). They vary continuously along the line and track α/ν. Wiseman and Domany call the Li–Sokal bound z ≥ α/ν “moderately sharp” for this algorithm, with anomalies at X4 (zχ ≈ 0.94–0.99, low against the exact 1, which they attribute to the multiplicative logarithm) and at X1. Against the exact α/ν, the zint,χ points lie on or above the curve except at X4.[1] This page makes no claim about the physical relaxation of any system on the line. For an indicator this means only that, for this algorithm, the strength of slowing down depends on where on the line one sits.

8Where else the line lives

The eight-vertex model. Ashkin–Teller maps to Baxter’s eight-vertex model, and its critical segment is isomorphic to the eight-vertex critical line (Wegner; Fan and Wu; Kadanoff).[1][7]

The c = 1 picture. Among the critical theories of central charge c = 1 are two lines, the circle line (the Gaussian model) and the orbifoldThe second of the two lines of c = 1 critical theories (the first is the circle line); a radius r labels points on each. line. Critical Ashkin–Teller runs along the orbifold line from r = 1 (Ising × Ising) to r = √2 (four-state Potts). The two lines meet at the Kosterlitz–Thouless point of the XY model, circle r = √2 = orbifold r = 1/√2.[3]

A frustrated Ising model in disguise. Jin, Sen and Sandvik’s J1–J2 Ising model on the square lattice is Ashkin–Teller critical for g = J2/|J1| ≥ g* = 0.67(1), with the four-state Potts point at g*. Its critical curve g ∈ [g*, ∞) corresponds one-to-one with the Ashkin–Teller segment, L/K running from 1 towards 0. At the Potts point the Binder crossing takes the universal value U* = 0.792(4). The same model shows the pseudo-first-order signals of §7c.[6]

Converting from Jin, Sen and Sandvik’s convention

They write H = −Σ(σσ + ττ + K′σσττ) at temperature T. In this page’s (K, L): their K′ is the ratio L/K, and 1/T is K. So their K′ = 0.95 is L/K = 0.95 here, and their K′ ∈ [1, 0) is L/K ∈ [1, 0).[6]

9What to remember

  • Between two critical Ising copies, the bilinear στ coupling (y = 7/4, relevant) gives no line; the four-spin εσετ coupling (y = 0, exactly marginal) gives a line.
  • The line is exact: Z = 1 − 2X, from X0 (Ising × Ising, α/ν = 0) to X4 (four-state Potts, α/ν = 1).
  • Along it ν, γ and α/ν slide; γ/ν = 7/4 and η = 1/4 are pinned. That is weak universality, and a real magnet violates it.
  • An order-parameter variance scales identically along the line; an energy variance does not.
  • Fits at ℓ ≤ 128 miss the exact α/ν, worst at the Potts end, where a multiplicative logarithm sits.
  • Double peaks and negative Binder minima appear at continuous transitions up to ℓ = 256.
